01

One foot in finance,one in research.

Where I sit, what I've done, and which of it is the actual through-line.

I like problems that sit between technical depth and a real business answer. Financial reporting is full of them: the number has to be right, and it has to mean something to whoever opens the file.

At SitusAMC the work runs on two separate tracks. With the Accounting team, on Accounting's data, I build executive reporting for Corporate IT, Global Prepaid and SG&A — 14.2M+ journal entries modeled into six reporting solutions spanning three business segments and 90+ products.

The second track is cloud cost, and it doesn't touch Accounting at all: a triage and owner-routing framework built for my own FinOps team and the Cloud Engineering team, plus vendor evaluation and writing things down so the next analyst doesn't rediscover them.

The through-line is analytics engineering. Get the grain and the pipeline right, then make the output legible. That's the same job whether the input is journal entries, twenty-five years of S&P 500 prices, or a seven-table hospital schema.

Before that I spent eight months on a research collaboration between USF and Florida Blue, fine-tuning Med42-8B for lung cancer decision support — synthetic corpus generation, QLoRA training, clinical validity review. It is the hardest thing I've built, and it's not the lane I work in day to day; it's just why a messy pipeline doesn't scare me.
